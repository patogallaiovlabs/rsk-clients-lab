# Prose rules

Apply all of these. They strip the register that makes text read as machine-written.

## Cut

- **Every em dash.** A period, a comma, a colon, or parentheses does the job.
- **Definitions by negation.** "not X, but Y", "isn't just a performance problem, it's a
  correctness problem", "less about A, more about B". State the positive claim and stop.
- **Openers that withhold the subject.** "What makes this interesting is...", "Here's the
  thing:", "The result?", "Enter Foundry." Start with what you are talking about.
- **Rhetorical questions used as transitions.** Keep the answer, drop the question.
- **Any clause you cannot source.** If the back half of a sentence exists to finish the
  sentence, you invented it.
- **Filler.** Delete each sentence and reread the paragraph. If nothing was lost, it was filler.

## Rewrite

- **Verbless fragments.** "Eight months. Every block. No divergence." is three fragments
  pretending to be emphasis.
- **Impressive words into spoken ones.** leverage becomes use, delve becomes look at,
  underscores becomes shows, robust usually becomes nothing at all. Technical terms stay.
- **Triads.** Three adjectives or three clauses in a row almost always means the third is
  there for rhythm and carries no information.
- **Uniform sentence length.** Three consecutive mid-length declaratives is the AI register,
  even when each sentence is fine alone. Vary length inside every paragraph.

## Structure

- One header per real division of the argument. Headers are not pacing.

## Check before finishing

- The edit made the text **shorter**. Count if unsure.
- No em dashes remain, including in titles and headers.
